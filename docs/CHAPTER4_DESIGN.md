# Chapter 4 · The Library of Unfinished Stories — design document (from the user)

**Design principle:** Pupu and Mishti stay exactly as they are in the game. Redesign only the worlds, supporting characters, environmental storytelling and challenges.

## 1. Vision
The chapter where Pupu's World becomes visually experimental: a rural Bangladeshi library whose books hold three story-worlds, each with its own visual language and core mechanic, each looking made from its book's material (painted paper, layered cut-outs, shadow puppetry). Emotional idea: these are stories Dadu once read to Pupu; their endings have vanished and Pupu must recover the words and complete the book.
**Core objective:** recover the missing words from all three stories, restore the blank storybook and discover its connection to Dadu.
**Pillars:** distinct worlds; different gameplay (river timing & moving platforms / stealth & observation / shadow platforms & lighting puzzles); environmental storytelling (incomplete bridges, missing illustration details, drifting letters); emotional payoff into Dadu's bedtime-story memory; readable platforming with sparse checkpoints.

| Sequence | Activity | Result |
|---|---|---|

## 2. Story
Secret room: after the radio memory Mishti notices an old cloth-bound book beneath the dusty console; familiar, but Pupu can't remember why. Blank pages except a few faint handwritten letters; a golden beam; letters swirl like fireflies and pull them in. Kalo-Bijli vows to find Pupu.
Arrival: they tumble out of a giant book onto a reading table: tin roof, whitewashed brick, tall almirahs, hurricane lamp, moonlight through a skylight. Three books glow, leaking letters in the same handwriting; Pupu gradually works out the words flew into them. Mishti wonders alongside, never explains.
What is happening: each book holds an unfinished story; characters repeat actions; worlds are incomplete (bridges stop halfway, trees lack leaves, houses miss walls, platforms are faint pencil sketches) and become complete as words return.
Kalo-Bijli: appears through an old radio, a flickering screen or a strange illustrated frame, trying to grab the glowing pages to power his machine: a nuisance, not the central threat.

## 3. Library hub
Small, playable, rewards exploration, connects the three worlds, compact (no long empty crossings).
Visuals: tin roof, wooden beams, whitewashed brick and worn plaster; uneven shelves (leaning, stacked); large central reading table with the blank storybook; hurricane lamps, dusty ceiling fan, broken wall clock, librarian's desk; newspapers tied with string, scattered books, wooden chairs, locked cabinet; skylight with moonlight through dust.
Areas: upper shelves (rolling ladder, manuscripts, hidden items: vertical exploration, secrets) · left shelf (river story) · right shelf/cabinet (village story) · lower shelf (theatre story) · reading table (book, lamp, pages: progress) · back wall (clock, notices: atmosphere, clues).
Props: rolling ladder along the shelves (clear stopping point, stable collision); book trolley (push under a shelf as a step); desk drawer (old letters / optional collectible); ceiling fan moves paper/dust; wall clock hands sometimes move backwards; wooden stool (movable step); locked cabinet (optional reward); skylight (later the kite route).

## 4. Story 1 · The Clever Jackal and the Crocodile
Timing, jumping, moving platforms, reading patterns. Hand-painted paper, layered river waves, illustrated foliage.
Environment: layered blue/teal paper waves, giant banyans with roots, oversized leaves, water lilies, logs, muddy islands, broken bamboo bridges, tall grass, banana plants, reeds, paper boats, fireflies, distant rooftops, faint pencil outlines of missing scenery.
Layout: A Riverbank (roots, ledges, broken bridge down to the water) · B Crocodile crossing (time jumps as crocodiles surface, submerge, move) · C Flooded forest (drifting logs, current direction) · D The jackal's trick (follow the story's trick to the page).
Mechanics: crocodile platforms (readable before committing), floating logs, hanging roots (optional higher routes), gentle currents shown by leaves/debris, paper bridges restored by nearby letters.
Characters: clever jackal (slim, long tail, big ears, pointed muzzle, warm orange-brown; playful, head tilts, tail gestures) · crocodile (broad, oversized jaw, short legs, big eyes, dark green scales; proud but gullible, slow turns, exaggerated reactions). Expressions tell the tale without dialogue.

## 5. Story 2 · Tuntuni and the Cat
Stealth, crouching, hiding, observation, sound. Colourful layered paper-cut village, visible folded edges, flat shapes, oversized objects.
Environment: thatched houses with layered paper walls and cut-out windows, bamboo fences, banana trees, a pond, rice fields, haystacks, earthen pitchers, hanging laundry, small bridges, baskets, clay pots, oversized flowers, paper birds and butterflies; scissor-cut edges.
Layout: A Quiet village (cat asleep; learn crouching/hiding) · B Cat's territory (haystacks, fences, banana plants; move while it patrols) · C Noisy kitchen (pots, utensils; don't disturb noisy objects) · D Rooftops (bamboo ladders, roof gaps, cat below) · E Tuntuni's escape (Tuntuni's distraction window).
Mechanics: obvious hiding spots; noisy objects the cat reacts to; readable attention (head turns, ears, sniffing, sight direction; no invisible detection); bamboo ladders (vertical and sideways); paper curtains to pass behind; pushable baskets/crates.
Characters: Tuntuni (tiny tailorbird, pointed beak, large eyes, colourful feathers; fast hops, wing flicks) · giant cat (oversized head and paws, whiskers, thick tail; heavy steps, slow patrols, sniffing, sudden attention shifts). Mishti can look nervous but never knows its weaknesses.

## 6. Story 3 · The Tiger and the Fox
Shadow platforms, light manipulation, timing, environmental puzzles. Traditional Bengali shadow-puppet theatre: dark stage, lit cloth screens, flat silhouettes.
Environment: cream cloth screen in a wooden frame, dark stage, oil lamps, lanterns, movable spotlights, bamboo supports, ropes, puppet boxes, painted curtains, folk-art patterns, wooden platforms, puppet rods and hanging shapes.
Palette: stage shadows #151925 · screen cloth #EBD9A8 · lamp light #E49A43 · muted warm brown wood · near-black silhouettes.
Layout: A Theatre entrance (light creates changing shapes) · B Shadow forest (jump safe shadow shapes) · C Moving spotlight (predict platforms appearing/disappearing) · D Final performance (two lights, right order).
Mechanics: shadows become solid when lit from the right angle (clear edges); levers/switches move lamps; giant puppets cross the screen and block routes; climb theatre supports, ropes, frames; final two-light puzzle.
Characters: tiger (big head, strong silhouette, long tail, bold stripes; slow deliberate puppet moves) · fox (pointed snout, bushy tail, thin legs, sharp ears; quick, deceptive poses). Flat silhouettes moved by rods.

## 7. The final page
Short emotional interaction: faint marks; three illustrated choices (help a lost character / return home / share what was found), all leading to the revelation; handwriting appears and the illustration forms; the cover reveals Dadu's handwritten dedication. Light shifts from teal to warm gold, music softens, a moment to look at the book.

## 8. Memory 4 · Dadu's bedtime stories
Traditional rural room at night: Dadu in an armchair by a hurricane lamp, little Pupu curled beside him, the storybook between them. Wooden armchair, low stool/floor cushion, hurricane lamp, old wooden chest, mosquito net, woven mat, wooden window, moonlight, small table with water glass and family photos.
Activities: 1 match the voices (jackal, bird, tiger/fox) · 2 turn the page when Dadu pauses or asks · 3 finish the story (little Pupu chooses an ending, echoing the final page). Ends with Dadu placing the book in the old wooden chest: Dadu's Storybook (4/10).

## 9. Bridge to Chapter 5 · The kite war
A paper kite's tail drifts through the skylight; wind grows: pages lift, books open, the lamp flickers, the storybook turns its pages; the kite rises and draws them to the spiral staircase and up to the roof; village rooftops under the moon.
End card: CHAPTER 4 COMPLETE – EVERY STORY HAS AN ENDING. Next: CHAPTER 5 – THE KITE WAR.

## 10. Art direction
Library: warm parchment, faded cream, dark teal, golden lamp light (aged wood, worn plaster, paper, dust, moonlight) · River: leaf green, river blue, muddy brown (painted paper, layered waves) · Village: bright greens, terracotta, mustard (cut paper, folds, scissor edges) · Theatre: deep navy, near-black, parchment cream, amber (silhouettes, cloth screen, wooden frames) · Memory: golden light, warm wood, muted brown.
Recurring magic: floating letters (subtle during hard jumps), flying pages lead the eye, missing parts as pencil sketches until restored, completed books stop leaking and gain colour, warm lamp pools against teal shadows.
Consistency: bold clean outlines, restrained cel shading, Pupu and Mishti unchanged, clear foreground/gameplay/background layers, strongest lighting reserved for entrances, restorations and the memory.

## 11. Ladders
Orientation decides the animation: front-facing (faces camera, alternating hands/feet), back-facing (back and shoulders visible), sideways (side profile, horizontal rungs: treat as horizontal traversal in 2D). Separate animation states, ladder metadata for orientation, snap to the climb path (no sliding), disable walking while attached, consistent climb speed matched to rung spacing, clear enter/exit at top and bottom, test with Pupu's real rig.

## 12. Priorities & build order
Very high: three distinct worlds, different mechanics, restoration & letters. High: hub with ladders, cat stealth, crocodiles, shadow platforms, supporting animations. Medium: letter effects, optional collectibles.
Order: block out the hub → prototype each mechanic in a small test room → lock palettes → build full levels → supporting characters → letters & restoration → final page & memory → kite transition, lighting, sound, polish.

## 13. Final direction
Three illustrated books opening inside one nostalgic library; gameplay changes with the art; the emotional centre is Pupu rediscovering his childhood and realising the stories Dadu told him are now his to finish.
